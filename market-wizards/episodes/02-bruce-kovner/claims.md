## Method, risk, psychology (all S01, Kovner's own statements — IC)
| ID | Claim |
|---|---|
| M01 | Two elements of success: imagining configurations of the world different from today and believing they can happen (soybeans doubling; dollar to 100 yen); staying rational and disciplined under pressure |
| M02 | Scenario method: assume today's price is correct, then look for changes that will alter it; form many mental pictures, try them on one at a time; most prove wrong; when nine out of ten elements click, that picture becomes the working view |
| M03 | Almost always trades on a market view; uses technical analysis a great deal; can't hold a position without understanding why it should move; technicals can clarify the fundamental picture |
| M04 | The market leads because better-informed participants vote first; the initial direction after a major development is often a good tip-off |
| M05 | Technical analysis has much that is right and much "mumbo jumbo"; it tracks the past, doesn't predict; it is a thermometer; it reflects the vote of the entire marketplace |
| M06 | Tight congestions that break out for reasons nobody understands are usually good risk/reward; breakouts on a widely read news story are less relevant (Heisenberg analogy) |
| M07 | The more a pattern is watched by speculators, the more false signals; computerised trend-following money produces false signals; he built similar systems to know when they would kick in |
| M08 | Predetermined stop before entry; the stop determines position size; stop set on a technical basis, beyond a barrier, not inside a range; avoid points floor traders reach easily |
| M09 | Stops are "never on the floor, but they are not mental" — organised so they get taken care of |
| M10 | A loss of money slows him down — he reduces positions; a technical breach (e.g., a major high penetrated) forces re-evaluation |
| M11 | Tries not to risk more than 1% of the portfolio on any trade; daily computer analysis of correlations; eight highly correlated positions = one position eight times as large; prefers long one market / short a related one |
| M12 | First rule: don't get caught in a situation where you can lose a great deal of money for reasons you don't understand |
| M13 | Be willing to make mistakes regularly: best judgment, wrong, next best, wrong, third best — and double your money (taught by Marcus) |
| M14 | Never bully a market; trying to push markets leads to arrogance, loss of touch, overtrading |
| M15 | If you personalise losses you can't trade; only poor money management disturbs him |
| M16 | Bear markets: sharp down moves and quick retracements; he sold too late and got stopped out in congestion; in bear markets use sharp counter-trend rallies to enter |
| M17 | Cross rates: fewer people watching; "the less observed, the better the trade"; violent, quick breakouts are much more reliable; the worse the fills, the better the trade |
| M18 | You need both fundamentals and technicals; fundamentals more important now; the 1970s were easier for pure technicians |
| M19 | Trend-following systems will kill each other off unless there is a period of high inflation |
| M20 | Stock market has more short-term countertrends; very long-term systems with very wide stops can catch big advances |
| M21 | 1987 crash: overly high prices plus portfolio-insurance selling; portfolio insurance was "insurance in name only" — a massive stop-loss order |
| M22 | Trading is close to zero-sum, so only a small group can be superior; hard work is essential; many one-year wonders |
| M23 | Advice to novices: risk management first; "undertrade, undertrade, undertrade"; cut intended size at least in half; novices trade 3–5× too big, risking 5–10% instead of 1–2% |
| M24 | Novices personalise the market; "I wish" and "I hope" take attention from the diagnostic process |
| M25 | Good trainees: "strong, independent, and contrary in the extreme"; disciplined about size; "A greedy trader always blows out" |
| M26 | Reads a daily "guru report" looking for a consensus the market is not confirming; following gurus makes conviction hard; praises Marty Zweig's risk control |
| M27 | Managed money is like a call option for the manager (asymmetric) — while stressing his reputation with investors matters |
| M28 | Trading as an intellectual game — a "multidimensional chess board"; e.g., working through a New Zealand finance minister's problems |
| M29 | Night coverage: staff 24 hours; call levels for every currency; a written scenario for every currency each week; assistant may wake him "twice a year" (a joke) |
