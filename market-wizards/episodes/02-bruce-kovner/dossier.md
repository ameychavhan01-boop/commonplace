## The 19 dimensions
1. **Biography & background** — New York-born, raised partly in Los Angeles; an academic and political operative before trading (C01–C06). Fiercely private; agreed to Schwager to "establish one accurate record" (C29).
2. **Early life & education** — Harvard; political science teaching at Harvard and Penn; left without finishing a doctorate (secondary sources) (C03–C04). The interview itself has no material on childhood — filled from S02 and flagged.
3. **Entry into markets** — a year of self-study; fell for interest-rate theory; exploited mispriced spreads in brand-new interest-rate futures (C07–C12).
4. **Career development** — $3,000 stake → soybean spread → near-ruin → Commodities Corporation (via Marcus) → Caxton (1983) → by 1988 perhaps the largest interbank currency trader (C23–C30).
5. **Philosophy** — markets as a multidimensional chess board; imagine many worlds and wait for confirmation (M01–M02, M28).
6. **Methodology** — scenario-based fundamental macro, cross-checked by price action; trades in currencies (50–60% of profits), bonds, commodities (C32, M02–M04).
7. **Technical & fundamental** — fundamentals first, but price is "a thermometer"; the market often knows first (M03–M05, C34).
8. **Markets & instruments** — interbank FX (incl. crosses), interest-rate futures, copper, coffee, soybeans, crude, T-bonds; avoids the IMM except for arbitrage (C32, C36–C37).
9. **Entry & exit** — buys unexplained breakouts from tight ranges; distrusts news-driven and crowded breakouts; predetermined technical stops beyond barriers (M06–M08, M17).
10. **Risk management** — exit before entry; organised stops; total portfolio risk measured daily; "don't get caught losing for reasons you don't understand" (M08–M12).
11. **Position sizing** — size set by the stop; ≤1% per trade; correlated positions aggregated; pairs (long one / short related) (M08, M11).
12. **Capital preservation** — 1977 near-ruin; 1981 −16% led to risk systems and daily total-risk measurement (C19–C20, C31, M16).
13. **Psychology** — sick after the soybean trade; don't personalise losses; emotional burden of daily million-dollar swings; enjoys the intellectual game (C19, M15, M28).
14. **Discipline & decision-making** — rationality under pressure; impulsive decisions are the danger (C18, M01); Schwager's summary on impulsive trades.
15. **Successes & failures** — successes: first interest-rate spread, soybean sixfold, 1987 dollar call, Canadian dollar, sterling/mark breakout; failures: copper spread too early, the 13 April 1977 decision, 1981.
16. **Historical market experiences** — 1977 soybean shortage; 1980–81 commodity bear market; 1987 crash; Louvre-era central-bank management of currencies; sterling's DM 3 cap.
17. **Lessons** — see Chapter 12 of outline.
18. **Strengths & weaknesses** — Strengths: broad intellectual synthesis, tight risk architecture, adaptability, humility about the market "knowing more". Weaknesses: method hard to replicate (Schwager); dependence on an extraordinary information network; size limits (copper "elephant"); later Caxton returns lower as assets grew (C45–C46).
19. **Relevance today** — risk per trade, correlation aggregation and stop-based sizing are universal; "less observed is better" more relevant with crowded systematic money; his 1988 view that trend-following would self-destruct is debatable (A10).
